# Line edit of essay.md (v4)

Scope: prose only, from the title to the end of Recommendation 10. Figures, alt text, the numbered questions in Recommendation 1 and the source list are excluded from the counts. Measured body: 40 paragraphs, 175 sentences, 4,215 words.

## 1. Overall diagnosis

The register is a regulator's review paper: correct, even, impersonal, with the stiffness of a law-firm memo ("accordingly", "thereafter", "ceases to be", "subject to written rules for the occasions on which"). The two earlier complaints were answered mechanically. The bold lead-ins went, but 16 of 40 paragraphs still open with a verdict of ten words or fewer and then cite, and at least eleven still close on a moral. The short sentences went, but they were welded together with ", and" (39% of sentences contain it) and ", so that"; the clauses inside the long sentences are still the same parallel assertions. Meanwhile the devices a briefing writer uses to turn and to land a point were stripped out: there is one "but" in 4,215 words, no sentence begins with But, Yet, So or And, there is one semicolon, no dash and no question. What is left is a three-key instrument of comma, colon and full stop. The content is that of an expert; several passages could only have been written by someone who knows payments. The voice is that of a careful summariser of other people's papers. The gap to partner-level prose is one substantial revision, mostly of sentence joints, paragraph endings and the placing of the author's judgment.

The dominant constructions:

- The additive compound. "X, and Y" or "X, Y, and Z", where each clause is an independent assertion: "Finished work sits idle, and the engineer who wrote it has moved on…"; "These are associations, and they should be read with care."; "The wider programme is a larger undertaking, and the published accounts are consistent about that." This is the previous draft's short sentences with the full stops changed to commas.
- The trailing result clause. "…, so that all the caution has to be applied to the whole release at once"; "…, so that when a release misbehaves the first task is…"; "…, so that switching it off thereafter is an untested change". "So that" properly marks purpose; here it marks consequence five times out of six, which is why it reads as translated. Use "so".
- The passive recommendation. "Deployment should be separated", "should be proven", "should be planned", "should be written", "should be agreed", "should be recruited", "should therefore be engaged", "should be generated". Nobody is told to do anything.
- The trailing adverbial triplet. "…release each behaviour on its own, to a few clients first, on evidence."; "…do the same thing routinely, for ordinary changes, with tooling that makes it cheap."; "That is learned from production, all at once, with the fuller answer arriving…".
- The relative-clause pile-up, almost always in sentences that carry a citation: "with a step in which … and with a visible … signal, of which its engineers remarked that"; "a result from which it drew no conclusion given the sample"; "a first ring, a separate instance for accounts that volunteered for the risk, on which every deployment would land first".
- Nominalisation is moderate, not endemic: "the rebuilding of the group's tests took", "the decision to enable the behaviour is taken", "the change in working practice it requires of", "gives the most candid account of the duration".
- "It is … that" does not occur. The feared bureaucratic fillers "namely", "in the course of", "in respect of" and "it remains the case that" do not occur either.

### Jargon used imprecisely

- "Release" does three jobs: the bundle on the train ("each release spends three days", "if the release is backed out"), the act of exposing behaviour ("release each behaviour on its own") and the event ("on the day of a release cut"). In a document whose thesis is that deploying and releasing are different things, the word must mean one thing. Use "train" or "build" for the bundle.
- The waiting period is a "soak" (10), a "hold" or "long hold" (8), a "bake" (1), a "canary" (5), "the three days" and "the promotion period". Pick "soak" for elapsed time under load and "promotion" for the movement between environments, and gloss "canary" once.
- A flag is "disabled", "off", "switched off" and, in a caption, "dormant"; exposure proceeds by "rung", "stage", "ring", "cohort", "wave" and "ramp". The ladder in Recommendation 3 has five rungs in the text and a different five in the figure's alt text.
- "Client" (28) gives way to "customer" (4), "user" (2) and "merchant" (1) whenever a source is paraphrased. Acceptable inside a source's story; say so once or normalise.
- "Develop substantially faster": "develop" is left intransitive. "Velocity" appears once, unprepared.
- "Stability" is never tied to a measure, although the FCA paragraph supplies one (change failure rate).

### British and American

Spelling is consistently British ("behaviour", "authorises", "programme", "penalises", "recognised"). The lapses:

- "percent" three times ("a quarter of a percent of users, then one percent", "one percent of them") beside "%" eleven times. British usage is "per cent"; better, use the figure: "0.25% of users, then 1%, then 5%".
- Curly quotation marks nested inside straight ones in the Netflix quotation.
- "one to twelve hours" beside "16 days", "14 days": settle on words to ten, figures above.
- Tense in the FCA paragraph: "published", "found", "said", then "notes".

## 4. Rhythm and emphasis

### The important word in a weak position

- The thesis of the document is in the passive tail of a 41-word sentence: "…while no client observes any difference, and the decision to enable the behaviour is taken separately and later."
- The mechanism of the whole benefit appears once, as the last sentence of the sixth paragraph of Recommendation 3: "The gain in velocity comes from freeing the ordinary changes that would otherwise have queued behind the risky one." It belongs in the Summary, where the reader is instead given "They develop substantially faster".
- "Release remains a deliberate act, applied to one behaviour at a time and to as few clients as the business chooses, and can be more careful than it is today." The claim that matters to a risk officer, that release becomes more careful, is an afterthought hung on ", and".
- "Of all changes, 1.6% failed. Of the changes that firms classed as major, which received additional oversight, 3.8% failed." Both sentences end on "failed"; the contrast is between the numbers and between "all" and "major", and neither is in the stress position.
- "More than six million customers were affected." The number leads and the sentence ends on a dead participle.
- "That is a reason to proceed by measured stages, as recommended below, and not a reason to stay where it is." The section ends on "it is", on the negative, after a cross-reference.
- "Emergency changes … led to a new incident 1.5% of the time, although the FCA notes that this may be partly because an incident was already open." The reader is never told what the figure is for, and the sentence ends on its own qualification.

### Short sentences that land

They exist and should be studied: "None of them asks for three days." "It should never be reused." "Scheme mandates take effect on the schemes' dates." Each comes after a long sentence and states a fact, not a moral.

## 5. Cohesion and logic

### Summary

Coherent, but paragraph 3 is a list (three passive "should be" sentences of 15, 21 and 16 words) introduced by a sentence about the document. The step from "three days" (sentence 1) to "roughly five days" (sentence 2) is unexplained until Finding 1.

### Finding 1

Sequential and tight. The Wednesday example cannot be checked by the reader because the train's days are never given.

### Finding 2

Paragraph 1 is four parallel effects, a list. Paragraph 2 is three citations in parallel plus a closer. The Amazon sentence supports "waiting dominates" and belongs with Recommendation 1, question 1; only LinkedIn and Facebook support the section's claim about behaviour.

### Finding 3

Paragraph 1 follows. Paragraph 2 is the best piece of sequential reasoning in the Findings, but it never names what it is doing: dismissing the obvious alternative.

### Finding 4

"Within the present model, then" is earned. The Google quotation is about canary duration against release frequency; it proves the previous section's second paragraph, not the coupling claim it is attached to. In paragraph 2, "This is established practice" is supported by a 2008 dark launch and a 2024 reference to canaries; neither is a feature flag, and the two are yoked by ", and" across sixteen years.

### Finding 5

The heading claims "Firms that separated the two kept their stability". The FCA evidence is about smaller, more frequent releases, not about separating deployment from release. The section slides between the two without saying so. The FCA paragraph is a list of six statistics with no sentence saying which matters. "Two further cases complete the picture" joins two unrelated cases; the Netflix case is evidence about soaks and belongs in Finding 3, where it would also resolve a tension the document never addresses: Finding 3 says the soak "does real work", Finding 5 says a long hold decays into "verifying endurance", and Recommendation 6 says "a soak would have proved as little".

### Finding 6

"The evidence therefore establishes that…": not earned. The two preceding sentences report what the author did not find; a positive conclusion cannot follow from them by "therefore". The honest connective is "What the evidence does show is…".

### Recommendation 1

Clean. The final sentence is the most honest in the document and one of the best.

### Recommendation 2

Paragraph 1 says deployment "changes nothing a client can see"; paragraph 2 opens by conceding that a build contains library upgrades and drivers that change a great deal. The concession should come first or the first claim should be limited to flagged behaviour.

### Recommendation 3

The worked example is properly sequential; this is where the document reasons instead of asserting. One unearned claim: "This supplies most of what the soak was expected to reveal". Finding 3 said the soak exists to reveal leaks, deepening queues and the date roll; a shadow comparison of routing decisions reveals none of them. The Uber and Basel paragraph is placed after the conclusion of the example and reads as an appendix.

### Recommendation 4

Two topics (the unit of exposure; consent and agreed dates) with no sentence joining them.

### Recommendation 5

"The business should therefore treat rollback as an ordinary operation": earned by RBS. The Google and Amazon sentences that follow are parallel citations. The last two sentences are the recommendation and arrive at the end.

### Recommendation 6

Paragraph 1: five parallel assertions, then two statistics joined by ", and". Paragraph 2 is sequential and strong. Paragraph 3 runs four topics together: the flag's metadata, the author's duties, Microsoft's experience and appraisal. "The business should expect the same resistance": what Microsoft describes is indifference, not resistance.

### Recommendation 7

"The recommendation therefore carries a condition" is earned. The second paragraph answers a question nobody has asked yet and ends by postponing it.

### Recommendation 8

The Starling paragraph does not support "relocated": both examples show approval retained. The second paragraph is properly argued, from the rule texts to the auditor's method to the co-authorship to Knight, and is the model for the rest.

### Recommendation 9

Three parallel cases, legitimately a list, closed by a sentence that sums them.

### Recommendation 10

Paragraph 1 is a checklist. In paragraph 2, "Separately, when executives…" admits that the sentence does not follow. In paragraph 3, "gives the most candid account of the duration" introduces two outage stories; duration arrives only in the last three sentences.

## 6. Voice and authority

### Where there is an expert

- The soak list: "sessions that fail only after hours of idleness, memory and file handles that creep, queues that deepen, the roll of the business date".
- "The choice of rail is made once, when the payment is accepted, and is stored on the payment".
- "never a fresh build from older source".
- "a flag can reverse a decision and cannot reverse a payment".
- "Auditors, however, test a firm against its own change policy, and that policy describes the process the firm has today."
- All of Recommendation 9.
- "The routing change itself reaches every client later under this model, perhaps by several weeks." Conceding the cost unprompted is what an expert does.

### Where there is a summariser

Finding 2 paragraph 2, Finding 5 throughout, Recommendation 7 paragraph 1, Recommendation 10 paragraphs 2 and 3. In these the grammatical subject of nearly every sentence is a source ("Facebook measured", "It found", "Netflix found", "OANDA spent", "It also records") and the author appears only in the closer.

The first person occurs five times. Once it recommends. The other four report the limits of the author's reading ("I have found no bank", "I found no account", "The rule texts I have read", "None of the accounts I read"). The "I" is used to disclaim and "this assessment" to conclude, which is the wrong way round.

### Moves that would supply authority

- Commit to numbers where the text now waves. "A small slice of production", "a few clients", "a small team", "substantially faster", "perhaps by several weeks", "years" (in a heading). If the business's data is needed first, give the range you would expect and say what would move it.
- Name the usual mistake as a mistake. Finding 3's second paragraph should begin: "The obvious fix is to run the train daily. It fails."
- Dismiss in a clause. "Shortening the soak, the other obvious fix, simply stops looking."
- Predict the reader's objection and answer it in the author's own voice: "Your auditors will say the policy forbids it. They are right; the policy is what has to change."
- Say which evidence you trust and which you do not, in the first person: "I put most weight on the FCA review and least on the vendors' accounts."
- State the recommendation before the evidence in every Recommendation section. At present 5, 7 and 8 open with other firms' stories.
- Allow one dry line per section and no more. The document has the material: a change board that rejected nothing all year; auditors who "loved" a chat message.

## 9. Style sheet for the next draft

1. Sentence length. Target a mean of 18 to 20 words and a median near 17. No more than one sentence in ten over 35 words, and none over 45 unless it is a true series. Between one in six and one in five at ten words or fewer.
2. Placement of short sentences. No more than one paragraph in four may open with a sentence under ten words. Short sentences belong after a long one, and they state a fact ("Nobody checked the eighth."), never a moral.
3. The ", and" budget. At most one sentence in five may join independent clauses with ", and". Before using it, ask what the relation is (cause, contrast, sequence, concession) and use the word for that relation.
4. Restore the turning words. "But", "yet", "so" and "nor" are permitted, including at the head of a sentence. Aim for a "but" or "yet" in every section that concedes something. One semicolon per paragraph is permitted where two clauses balance.
5. "So that" for purpose only. Use "so" for consequence. Ban "whereas", "thereafter", "accordingly", "ceases", "constitutes", "warrants", "obliges" and "subject to".
6. Ban "X and not Y". Write "X rather than Y", or state Y first and turn with "but". No more than two negative-then-positive pairs in the whole document.
7. Recommendations in the imperative. Each Recommendation section opens with the instruction ("Ship every material change with its flag off."), then the reason, then the evidence, then the condition or exception. "The business should" at most once per section. No passive "should be" without an agent.
8. First person for judgment. Replace "this assessment finds" with "I". Use "I" at least once per Finding to say which evidence carries weight, not only to report what was not found.
9. One source per sentence. The source is the grammatical subject at most once per paragraph. After that, give its findings as facts and let the footnote carry the attribution. Never gloss the source's caution in the same sentence as its finding.
10. Quotations. Keep a quotation only when the wording is the evidence ("raises questions over the effectiveness of CABs", "our auditors loved this one"). Put it at the end of its sentence, introduce it with a colon or a plain verb, and never with "of which … remarked that". A quotation whose sense is unclear out of context ("all customers are affected or none") is paraphrased.
11. Statistics. No more than two figures in a sentence and four in a paragraph. Every figure is followed, in the same or the next sentence, by what it shows. Use "%" with numerals throughout; never "percent".
12. One term for one thing. "Train" for the bundle; "release" only for exposing behaviour to clients; "soak" for elapsed time under load; "ramp" for staged exposure; "off" for a flag's state. Define "material change" at first use by a test a director could apply.
13. No signposts. Delete any sentence whose only content is that evidence follows or that a case "shows" something ("Two further cases complete the picture", "An example shows what each rung contributes"). Start with the case.
14. Endings. A paragraph ends on its most specific fact or on the instruction, never on a restatement, an exception, a cross-reference or a paired abstraction ("openly and early"). At most one section in three may end on a generalisation. Exceptions and qualifications go in the middle of the paragraph.
15. Earned connectives and committed quantities. Every "therefore" must follow from the sentence immediately before it; check each by reading the two sentences alone. Every "small", "few", "substantially" and "several" is replaced by a number or a range, or by a statement of what the number depends on.

## 10. The paragraph closest to the standard

Recommendation 9:

> Some changes should stay on the present model. A ledger schema change cannot be hidden behind a flag: the schema must be widened, written both ways, backfilled, compared and switched before it is narrowed, and since the narrowing is irreversible it warrants a rehearsal on a production-sized copy. A new settlement file reaches a counterparty whole, at a cut-off, and cannot be sent to one percent of them, so old and new should be generated in parallel through several cycles, including a period end, and compared. Scheme mandates take effect on the schemes' dates. None of these is a candidate for a ramp.

It opens by committing to a position against the grain of the document's own argument, which is what gives a consultant credibility. Every sentence carries knowledge that cannot be had from reading other firms' blog posts: the order of a schema migration, the fact that a file arrives "whole, at a cut-off", the period end. No source is cited because none is needed. The lengths are 8, 40, 38, 8 and 9 words, and both long sentences earn their length by narrating a procedure in order. "Scheme mandates take effect on the schemes' dates" is the driest and best short sentence in the document. Its faults are small: "warrants", "one percent", and a closing sentence that sums up where it could simply stop. Finding 1 and the second paragraph of Recommendation 8 are the runners-up, for arithmetic that proceeds in order and for an argument in which each sentence depends on the last.
