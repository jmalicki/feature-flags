# Parallel traditions and public codification

Research date: 2026-11-10. Primary sources inspected by the lead researcher. Publication dates are distinguished from dates of the practices described. These notes supplement the company dossiers; they do not establish an inventor of flags.

| Source | Date | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael Cusumano and Richard Selby, [How Microsoft Builds Software](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~atif/Teaching/Spring2003/cusumano.pdf), CACM 40(6) | June 1997 | Original field research describes daily synchronization alongside milestone stabilization, internal/external testing, and manufacturing release. Important corrective: frequent integration predates web companies. Figure 2 and pp. 54-57. |
| Martin Fowler and Matthew Foemmel, [Continuous Integration, original version](https://martinfowler.com/articles/originalContinuousIntegration.html) | September 10, 2000 | Automated builds/tests and daily integration; explicitly acknowledges Microsoft's daily builds. CI does not require production deployment. |
| Greg Linden, [Early Amazon: Shopping cart recommendations](https://glinden.blogspot.com/2006/04/early-amazon-shopping-cart.html) | April 25, 2006 | First-person retrospective: live measurement overcame executive opposition to cart recommendations. Establishes experimentation culture, not a precise event date or dormant-code platform. |
| Ron Kohavi, Randal Henne, Dan Sommerfield, [Practical Guide to Controlled Experiments on the Web](https://exp-platform.com/Documents/GuideControlledExperiments.pdf), KDD | August 2007 | Sections 3.3.1 and 5.2.2 recommend gradual treatment ramp and automated abort to zero for harmful results. Operational containment was already part of experimentation literature. |
| Timothy Fitz, [Continuous Deployment at IMVU](https://timothyfitz.com/2009/02/10/continuous-deployment-at-imvu-doing-the-impossible-fifty-times-a-day/) | February 10, 2009 | Extensive automated tests, subset deployment, regression monitoring and automatic rollback accompanied about fifty daily deployments. This post primarily proves deployment automation, not flags. |
| John Allspaw and Paul Hammond, [10+ Deploys Per Day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LdOe18KhtT4), Velocity | June 2009 | Primary recording is a viewing-list item. [Speaker-hosted slides](https://paulhammond.org/2009/06/velocity/10deploys.pdf) exceeded browser fetch limit; recording transcript not independently inspected. Detailed claims rely on readable Flickr accounts below. |
| Ross Harmes, [Flipping Out](https://code.flickr.net/2009/12/02/flipping-out/) | December 2, 2009 | Mainline integration and several daily deployments coexist with unfinished production-disabled features; environment flags and per-user flippers separate access. Explicitly discusses cleanup and residual hazards. |
| Eric Ries, [Continuous deployment for mission-critical applications](https://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2009/12/continuous-deployment-for-mission.html) | December 28, 2009 | Explains engineering/marketing release separation and links directly to Flickr's flags account. This is explicit circulation of the idea, not proof of its first invention. |
| Risk Olson, [How we deploy new features](https://github.blog/news-insights/the-library/how-we-deploy-new-features/) | July 8, 2010; page updated January 4, 2019 | Full short article available in search extraction; direct open intermittently failed. Explicitly cites Flickr and describes runtime Redis configuration and percentage traffic to new download server. Original text cannot be presumed wholly unchanged since 2010. |
| Jez Humble, [Continuous Delivery vs Continuous Deployment](https://continuousdelivery.com/2010/08/continuous-delivery-vs-continuous-deployment/) | August 13, 2010 | Distinguishes always-ready delivery from deploying every good build; automation and production-like/capacity/security testing remain essential. |
| Martin Fowler, [Feature Flag](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/FeatureFlag.html) | Originally October 29, 2010; revised 2016 and 2023 | Explains how flags reconcile mainline integration with longer feature work. Use original date for publication only; present-day taxonomy/advice cannot all be backdated to 2010. |
| John Allspaw, [Convincing management that cooperation and collaboration was worth it](https://www.kitchensoap.com/2012/01/05/convincing-management-that-cooperation-and-collaboration-was-worth-it/) | January 5, 2012, reproducing a late-2009 letter | Explicit Flickr-to-Etsy career bridge. Describes hidden backend calls and percentage load, with launch preparedness and shared availability responsibility. Retrospective introduction is distinguished from reproduced letter. |
| Netflix Technology Blog, [Preparing the Netflix API for Deployment](https://netflixtechblog.com/preparing-the-netflix-api-for-deployment-786d8f58090d) | November 18, 2013 | Independently inspected; also U04. Acceptance testing could continue with new functionality production-disabled. Unit/contract/integration tests and canary analysis retained. |
| Edith Harbaugh, [LaunchDarkly raises $2.6 Million](https://launchdarkly.com/blog/launchdarkly-raises-2-6-million-for-feature-flags-as-a-service/) | June 4, 2015 | Contemporary company announcement positions hosted flags as access to large-company capabilities. Evidence of commercialization, not independent validation of product benefits. |
| Edith Harbaugh, [First year in review](https://launchdarkly.com/blog/feature-flags-dark-launches-and-canary-releases-for-all-launchdarkly-first-year-in-review/) | July 15, 2015 | Names Flickr, Facebook, Etsy and others as antecedents. Contradictory first-workday dates within post; omit exact founding day. Vendor chronology of Facebook is less precise than implementer sources. |
| Pete Hodgson, [Feature Toggles](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html) | Serialized January-February 2016; wording revised October 2017 | Distinguishes release, experiment, operations, and permission toggles, and discusses carrying costs. Revision log establishes original 2016 publication despite 2017 display date. |
| Rahman, Querel, Rigby, Adams, [Feature Toggles: Practitioner Practices and a Case Study](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~pcr/paper/Rahman2016MSR.pdf), MSR | May 2016 | Original empirical research studies 39 Chrome releases and toggle maintenance. Supports benefits and debt, not a controlled causal proof that flags improve reliability everywhere. |

## Lead-researcher verification notes

- The Microsoft paper is direct observational research, rather than a participant blog; it is a primary study of the process.
- No external transcripts were silently substituted for watched talks. Talk abstracts, recordings and slides are distinguished from inspected article bodies.
- Google/Facebook are central examples, but the available record supports converging traditions and several explicit diffusion links rather than a single chain of descent.
