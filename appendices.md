## Appendix A: Regulation of software change

The notes below summarize what each text says about software change, as of November 2026. They are not legal advice. No supervisor, examiner or external auditor has published a view on how flags, canaries or ramps fit these texts, so where a text is silent the reading given is the author's.

### What regulators and auditors accept

The texts below ask, besides the requirements listed under [What the rules require](essay.md#what-the-rules-require), for separation of production from development and an audit trail. None of them asks for a soak period, a release train or promotion over several days.

| Text | Scope | What it asks of a change | What it accepts or says about frequent change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201) | SOX audits of internal control | Effective general controls over program changes; prescribes no procedure | An auditor relies on a baseline according to "the strength of the company's program change controls" and whether "the application is stable (i.e., there are few changes from period to period)" (.47, .B29, .B31), so frequent change raises the evidence needed |
| AICPA [Trust Services Criteria](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/download/2017-trust-services-criteria-with-revised-points-of-focus-2022), CC8.1 | SOC 2 reports | "The entity authorizes, designs, develops or acquires, configures, documents, tests, approves, and implements changes" | Points of focus, not mandatory, include authorization "prior to development", testing and approval "prior to implementation", and an emergency path |
| [ISAE 3402](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2023-2024-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related) (IAASB) and [SOC 1](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-1) (AICPA) | A service organization's report on its controls, for clients whose financial reporting the service affects and for their auditors | Control objectives and controls are stated by the service organization; a type 2 report covers a period, ordinarily at least six months | The description must include "relevant details of changes to the service organization's system during the period" (ISAE 3402, para. 16(b)); a shorter period is acceptable where "significant changes have been made to the controls" (A30) |
| [ISO/IEC 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001), Annex A 8.32 | Information security management | Change management procedures | Does not specify the procedure |
| [UN Regulation No. 156](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:42021X0388) | Vehicle software updates | A process to "confirm the compatibility of a software update with the target vehicle(s) configuration before it is issued" (7.1.1.7); configuration recorded before and after | Over the air, the ability to "restore systems to their previous version in case of a failed or interrupted update" or to place the vehicle in a safe state (7.2.2.1.1); no cadence or soak |
| FDA [guidance on predetermined change control plans](https://www.fda.gov/media/166704/download), December 2024, revised August 2025 | AI-enabled medical device software | A plan describing modifications, a modification protocol and an impact assessment, saying whether updates are automatic or manual | Nonbinding; neither requires nor prohibits a staged rollout |
| [DORA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32022R2554) and its [technical standards](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774) | EU banks, payment institutions, investment firms | Binding change management; see [Constraints in the EU standards](#constraints-in-the-eu-standards) | Applies ["regardless of the ICT project management methodology chosen by the financial entity"](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774); permits testing in production as an approved exception, attributed to "current ICT system development practices" |
| [FFIEC examiners' handbook](https://business.cch.com/BFLD/FFIEC-IT-DevelopmentAcquisitionMaintenance-Hankbook-08292024.pdf), 2024 | US bank examinations | Quality assurance independent of development | Mentions canary releases; "in a DevOps environment, the same number and type of changes can be made daily or even hourly" |
| MAS [technology risk guidelines](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/MAS/Regulations-and-Financial-Stability/Regulatory-and-Supervisory-Framework/Risk-Management/TRM-Guidelines-18-January-2021.pdf) | Singapore financial institutions | A change advisory board, signed-off test results, independent quality assurance | The only financial text here that expects a change advisory board and signed-off test results |
| ACPR, [2018 paper](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/system/files/import/acpr/medias/documents/818013_acpr_risque-informatique_web_fr_v3.pdf) and [2021 notice](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/system/files/import/acpr/media/2021/07/08/20210707_notice_risque_it.pdf) | French banks | Control over changes; the 2018 paper described, without requiring, change policies that grouped releases into batches outside service hours | In 2021 accepted agile methods and automated change processes, provided automation does not reduce control |
| BaFin [notice on cloud outsourcing](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Anlage/Aufsichtsmitteilung/dl_2024_02_01_Aenderung_Orientierungshilfe_Cloud.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=4), 2024 | German supervised firms | Manual review on a risk basis | CI/CD pipelines and automated tests can support segregation of duties |

None of the financial texts mentions feature flags or staged rollouts, and only the US handbook mentions canary releases.

The Dutch markets supervisor, the AFM, published [ten principles for change management](https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2024/changemanagement-overkreditering.pdf) at consumer and mortgage lenders in 2024, after errors in automated credit checks, which it said could [often be traced to changes in those IT systems](https://www.afm.nl/en/sector/actueel/2023/december/kredietaanbieders-acceptatieprocessen), had required remediation for more than 10,000 consumers. Alongside approval by at least two people, separated systems and a test plan, whose incompleteness the AFM found at the root of most incidents, the principles ask lenders to monitor deviations directly after a change, automatically where possible. They also ask lenders to consider running the changed system in parallel with the old one as a fallback, and suggest as good practice checking the first new loans by hand. The AFM also warns that falling back to old lending norms can itself cause over-lending, in which case it is better to hold orders until the error is fixed.

Supervisors' own data shows how much of their incident load comes from change. The ECB has reported [since 2023](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/press/publications/newsletter/2023/html/ssm.nl231115.en.html) that IT changes are a leading cause of downtime of critical services, and in 2024 described them as ["still by far the most important reason for downtime"](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/annex/ssm.nl241113_4_annex.en.pdf) at the banks it supervises. A member of its Supervisory Board [wrote in 2026](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/press/interviews/date/2026/html/ssm.in260324_5~3d0837ff0e.en.html) that "38% of the major incidents reported by banks in 2025 had 'IT change' as their root cause", and the ECB's [priorities for 2026 to 2028](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/framework/priorities/pdf/ssm.supervisory_priorities202511.en.pdf) include a targeted review of ICT change management.

A supervisor's response to an incident can nevertheless be less change, and some assessment tools assume the traditional process. After a series of outages at DBS in 2023, Singapore's regulator, MAS, [directed the bank to suspend changes to its IT systems](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2023/mas-imposes-six-month-pause-on-dbs) for six months, apart from those needed for security, regulatory compliance and risk management. The ECB's [IT risk questionnaire](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/activities/srep/2025/html/ssm.srep_ITRQ2025.en.pdf) asks whether significant changes are approved by a dedicated management committee and deployed only by authorized production teams. A bank that deploys through a pipeline should expect to explain how the pipeline fills that role, using its failure rate and the reach of each change as evidence. Where the criteria for pre-approved changes are loose, risky changes pass through the simplified route, which Norway's supervisor warned about in its [2026 risk report](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/publikasjoner-og-analyser/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026).

### Constraints in the EU standards

Several provisions in the EU standards affect how the model has to be built, and comparable expectations appear in other jurisdictions.

On approval, [Article 17(1)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774) requires "mechanisms to ensure the independence of the functions that approve changes and the functions responsible for requesting and implementing those changes". The standards do not define a function, and BaFin notes in its [guidance on DORA](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/Anlage/dl_2024_07_08_Aufsichtsmitteilung_Umsetzungshinweise_DORA_en.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3) that the provision "does not describe or require the implementation of a concrete process". A reasonable reading is that an approver who neither requested nor wrote the change satisfies it, with the pipeline as the implementer.

[Article 8(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774) permits testing in production only where the instances "are clearly identified, reasoned, are for limited periods of time," and are "approved by the relevant function", and Article 16 restricts the use of production data outside production. No supervisor has said whether a canary or a ramp step counts as testing in production. A firm can document each change as tested before deployment and treat the ramp as the managed transition that Article 17(1)(c) asks for, or it can record each ramp as an approved instance under Article 8(2), and its change policy should say which.

The standards cover "all changes to software, hardware, firmware components, systems, or security parameters", with no threshold of materiality, so a flag change in production is a change. Change procedures must include "procedures and responsibilities for aborting changes or recovering from changes not successfully implemented" (Article 17(1)(e)), error handling must include "rollback" (Article 8(2)(c)(iii)), and emergency changes may be approved "after their implementation" (Article 17(1)(g)).

### European Union

- DORA, [Regulation (EU) 2022/2554](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32022R2554), applies from January 17, 2025. Its technical standards on ICT risk management, [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774), contain the change-management requirements: independent approval (Article 17(1)(b)), fall-back and aborting changes (Article 17(1)(e)), emergency changes approved after implementation (Article 17(1)(g)), separation of production from development and testing in production as an approved, time-limited exception (Article 8(2)), rollback in error handling (Article 8(2)(c)(iii)), and restrictions on production data in non-production environments (Article 16). Smaller entities follow a simplified framework (Article 38) without the independence clause.
- The EBA's [guidelines on ICT and security risk management](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Guidelines/2020/GLs%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management/872936/Final%20draft%20Guidelines%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management.pdf) set change-management expectations from 2020. The EBA [deleted that section](https://eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-05/c8fb6168-0aef-42f6-8da9-114a8baf21b9/Consolidated%20version%20of%20EBA%20ameding%20Guidelines%20on%20ICT%20and%20security%20risk%20management.pdf) for firms within DORA's scope from May 20, 2025.
- Under DORA's incident rules, [Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1772), an incident affecting critical services is major when it meets two or more thresholds, or when it involves malicious unauthorized access that may cause data loss (Article 8(1)). The client thresholds are more than 10% of the clients using the service, more than 100,000 clients, or any client identified as relevant (Article 9(1)); the others include a duration of more than 24 hours from when the incident began, more than two hours of downtime for a service supporting critical or important functions, any impact on data integrity that harms business objectives or compliance (Article 9(5)(a)), and costs over EUR 100,000 (Article 9(6)). Recurring incidents with the same root cause within six months are aggregated. A small ramp stage therefore does not keep a defect below the thresholds at a firm with few large clients, where one client can be more than 10% of a service's clients or be identified as relevant, and a defect that damages data integrity can meet that threshold whatever its exposure.
- For algorithmic trading, [RTS 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32017R0589) requires predefined limits before an algorithm is deployed (Article 8), immediate "kill functionality" (Article 12) and a person designated by senior management to authorize deployment (Article 5(2)).
- The [Instant Payments Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R0886) requires instant credit transfers to be receivable "24 hours a day and on any calendar day", with penalties waived only for short, foreseeable, pre-announced maintenance.

### European Central Bank

- The ECB's [IT risk questionnaire](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/activities/srep/2025/html/ssm.srep_ITRQ2025.en.pdf) also asks whether a release management team exists. Its [2020 methodology report](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.aroutcomesrepitriskquestionnaire202007~9ed9aaa17d.en.pdf) scores the inherent risk of IT change by the frequency of changes to critical systems.
- A July 2026 [letter on AI-enabled cyber threats](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/press/letterstobanks/shared/pdf/2026/ssm.2026_letter_on_AI_enabled_cybersecurity_threats.en.pdf) refers to change management that enables "rapid, risk-based remediation while maintaining operational stability", in the context of patching.

### Germany

- BaFin's circular on bank IT, [BAIT](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/Rundschreiben/dl_rs_1710_ba_BAIT_en.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6), expected testers independent of developers, acceptance tests by business units and plans for reverting to an earlier version, and allowed lighter process rules for low-risk configuration and parameter changes. BaFin [removed banks within DORA's scope](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Anlage/dl_250110_Informationsschreiben.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1) from BAIT with effect from January 17, 2025, and BAIT lapses entirely at the end of 2026.

### Other European supervisors

- Italy: the Bank of Italy's [Circular 285](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Circolare-Banca-dItalia-n.-285-del-17-dicembre-2013-Aggiornamento-n.-40-del-2-novembre-2022.pdf) placed ICT change under a unit independent of development until its [51st update](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Atto-di-emanazione-del-51mo-aggiornamento-Banca-dItalia-03-febbraio-2026.pdf) of February 2026 repealed those sections in favor of DORA.
- Netherlands: De Nederlandsche Bank's [Good Practice Information Security 2023](https://www.dnb.nl/media/13jpibjp/good-practice-ib-2023-eng.pdf), which it describes as non-mandatory, recommends that no one requests, approves and implements a change to a critical system alone, that the impact analysis of a change take a fallback scenario into account, and that access to test and production systems be strictly separated, and it names DevOps alongside ITIL, Agile and Scrum. DNB has said that DORA replaces this guidance for firms within DORA's scope from January 17, 2025. The AFM's 2024 [change-management principles for consumer and mortgage lenders](https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2024/changemanagement-overkreditering.pdf), which also reach lenders outside DORA's scope, are summarized under [what regulators and auditors accept](#what-regulators-and-auditors-accept). For trading algorithms the AFM has written that having kill functionality alone is not enough and that firms should define a trading algorithm consistently across development, testing and deployment.
- Sweden: Finansinspektionen [fined Swedbank SEK 850 million](https://fi.se/en/published/news/2023/20232/swedbank-receives-a-remark-and-an-administrative-fine) in March 2023 for a 2022 change made "without following internal procedures", which left almost one million customers with incorrect balances.
- Norway: Finanstilsynet's [2026 risk report](https://www.finanstilsynet.no/publikasjoner-og-analyser/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026/risiko--og-sarbarhetsanalyse-2026) traces many of the 313 ICT incidents reported in 2025 to changes.
- Switzerland: [FINMA's circular on operational risks](https://www.finma.ch/en/~/media/finma/dokumente/dokumentencenter/myfinma/rundschreiben/finma-rs-2023-01-20221207.pdf) requires separate development and test environments and testing based on criticality.

### Model and data rules

In the United States, SR 26-2, issued in April 2026, replaces SR 11-7 ([SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm)). Its attachment states that "Validation generally occurs prior to a model's first use.", and the words "parallel", "champion" and "challenger" do not appear in it ([SR 26-2 attachment](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf)). The UK PRA's SS1/23 asks banks to compare development output "with the output of available challenger models" where practicable, and to compare output before and after a material change against actual outcomes, which it calls "parallel outcomes analysis" ([PRA SS1/23](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2023/ss123.pdf)). The [ECB](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.supervisory_guide_egim_202606.en.pdf) and the [EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/documents/files/document_library/Final%20Report%20on%20Big%20Data%20and%20Advanced%20Analytics.pdf) treat a parallel run that takes no decisions as expected or as good practice before sign-off, and in Europe a live challenger arm also carries customers' rights over automated decisions and the change controls of the EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA). None of these texts defines or forbids a traffic-split experiment. The AFM has [described banks and insurers running A/B tests on customers that last only a few days](https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2025/rapport-verkenning-toepassing-gedragsinzichten-banken-verzekeraars.pdf), and its concerns were with their method and the reading of their results, while it raised none about the practice itself.

BCBS 239 accepts more than one data model where there are "robust automated reconciliation procedures where multiple models are in use" ([BCBS 239](https://www.bis.org/publications/201301-guidelines-principles-effective-risk-data-aggregation-and-risk-reporting.pdf)), which is the closest any banking standard comes to accepting two schemas side by side. The ECB's 2024 guide on risk data aggregation expects "complete and up-to-date data lineages on data attribute level", automated quality controls with tolerances, and governance involvement in material changes, and names no deployment method ([ECB](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.supervisory_guides240503_riskreporting.en.pdf)).

For internal ratings-based (IRB) capital models, Delegated Regulation 529/2014 requires notification at least two months before implementation for a "change of the data sources used in the process of allocating exposures to grades or pools or for parameter estimation" ([529/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32014R0529)), so swapping the upstream table under an IRB model is a notifiable change even if the numbers do not move. The ECB's guide to internal models expects a bank applying for a material change to replicate the model and the capital calculation in "a parallel version of a live production environment that is already in place" ([ECB](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.supervisory_guide_egim_202606.en.pdf)). The EBA's 2023 report states that the change framework "does not constrain the use of self-training and self-development model challengers" while the rating system used for capital is unchanged ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document_library/Publications/Reports/2023/1061483/Follow-up%20report%20on%20machine%20learning%20for%20IRB%20models.pdf)), and its 2020 report describes an optional period in which a challenger "runs in parallel with the old system to enable a comparison of the results" ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/documents/files/document_library/Final%20Report%20on%20Big%20Data%20and%20Advanced%20Analytics.pdf)).

The EU AI Act treats credit scoring of natural persons as high-risk, excluding systems used to detect financial fraud ([AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689)), and Regulation 2026/1744 moved those obligations to "2 December 2027" ([2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32026R1744)). A change that affects compliance and was not foreseen in the initial conformity assessment is a substantial modification and needs a new assessment, so a model retrained on a new upstream schema with redefined features is likely to need one unless the change plan foresaw it, although the regulation does not say so directly.

No EU text forbids serving two credit models to different applicants, and each applicant keeps the GDPR Article 22 rights of the subject of a solely automated decision, including human intervention ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)). The CJEU held in SCHUFA that a credit score is such a decision where a lender "draws strongly on that probability value" ([SCHUFA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0634)), and from November 20, 2026 the Consumer Credit Directive entitles consumers to an explanation of the logic of an automated creditworthiness assessment ([CCD](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32023L2225)). The explanation has to describe the model that actually scored each applicant, so in practice the model version must be logged with each decision.

For fraud models, the PSD2 standard on strong customer authentication lets a provider exempt low-risk remote transactions only while its fraud rate over a rolling 90 days stays at or below reference rates (for remote card payments, 0.13% up to EUR 100, 0.06% up to EUR 250 and 0.01% up to EUR 500, and for remote credit transfers 0.015%, 0.01% and 0.005% in the same bands), and a provider above the rate for two consecutive quarters "shall immediately cease to make use of the exemption" ([Delegated Regulation 2018/389](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018R0389)). A challenger's fraud counts in the same provider-wide rate, so a worse challenger on a share of traffic puts the exemption at risk for every customer in that band, a consequence the regulation does not spell out, and an experiment with a fraud challenger should be capped in size for that reason.

The DORA standard on ICT risk management adds one constraint specific to data. Its [Article 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1774) requires that "non-production environments only store anonymised, pseudonymised, or randomised production data", so an unanonymised clone of a production table used for validation elsewhere needs a derogation, and a shadow run in production may need the approval that Article 8 of DORA's technical standards describes, although the standard addresses neither case directly. Under the incident classification standard, an impact on the integrity of data is one criterion for a major incident ([RTS 2024/1772](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32024R1772)).

Dutch supervisors' model guidance is principles-based. DNB's 2019 [principles for the use of AI](https://www.dnb.nl/media/jkbip2jc/general-principles-for-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-financial-sector.pdf) suggest documented criteria for a significant change and comparing material AI models with conventional ones, and the AFM's [2023 study of trading algorithms](https://www.afm.nl/~/profmedia/files/rapporten/2023/report-machine-learning-trading-algorithms.pdf) asks when a retrained algorithm becomes a new algorithm.
