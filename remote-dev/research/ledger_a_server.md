## S1. The laptop as a development machine

### S1.1 What the laptop costs in speed

1. Slack: a fresh clone of the main web application took nearly 30 minutes, and remote environments cut new-engineer setup from about an hour to minutes. Slack; 28 Jun 2022; [Slack Engineering](https://slack.engineering/remote-development-at-slack/); official, named author (Sylvestor George); V-RAW. Quote: "today a fresh clone takes nearly 30 minutes". Caveat: applies to Slack's "webapp" codebase.
2. Slack: running HHVM made laptops loud and slowed other applications; Homebrew installs and upgrades broke local setups. Slack; 28 Jun 2022; same link; official; V-RAW. Caveat: the post describes no mandate to switch; its "no mandated single development environment" refers to editor choice.
3. GitHub: bootstrapping github.com in a fresh environment took upwards of 45 minutes before shallow clones (about 5 minutes) and prebuilt pools (about 10 seconds). GitHub; 11 Aug 2021; [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/infrastructure/githubs-engineering-team-moved-codespaces/); official, named author (Cory Wilkerson); V-RAW. Caveat: GitHub sells Codespaces; the post is also a product launch.
4. Uber: on laptops, cloning and setting up a project remotely "would take hours"; Devpods gave Go builds about 2.5x the speed of laptops on longer builds and 1.8x on average builds, and p95 `git status` under 4 seconds on a monorepo of over 70 million lines. Uber; Dec 2022; [Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/en-US/blog/devpod-improving-developer-productivity-at-uber/); official; V-RAW (reader). Quote: "Devpods provide a 2.5X improvement over laptops on average for longer, more complex builds". Caveat: Uber's own benchmark; publication day (13 Dec) not seen on the page, a Hacker News submission of 14 Dec 2022 fits it.
5. LinkedIn: initial setup and build fell from 10 to 30 minutes to about 10 seconds for most products; the stated laptop problems are fewer cores, less memory and disk, and thermal throttling. LinkedIn; 4 Nov 2021; [LinkedIn Engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/cloud-computing/building-in-the-cloud-with-remote-development); official, named authors; V-RAW. Quote: "laptops typically have fewer CPU cores, less memory and disk space, and suffer from thermal throttling".
6. LinkedIn measured that video calls added a 2 to 4 times increase in latency to common developer operations on a laptop. LinkedIn, reported by a newsletter; 5 Sep 2023; [Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/cloud-development-environments-why-now); press (free preview); V-RAW. Caveat: second-hand; no LinkedIn document found for the measurement.
7. Shopify: before cloud development, local environments had outgrown the machine. Shopify; 9 Jun 2022; [Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/shopifys-cloud-development-journey); official, named author (Don Kelly); V-RAW. Quote: "Laptops were melting."
8. Shopify, agent era: three or four parallel agent workflows were enough to max out a laptop, and closing the lid stopped the work; first compile and checkout took over 30 minutes. Shopify; 22 Oct 2026; [Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/remote-dev); official; V-RAW. Quote: "Just three or four workflows were enough to max out a laptop". Caveat: this is mobile (iOS and Android) development.
9. Stripe, environment drift: laptops run tool versions years apart, and machines reappear with old versions after long absences. Stripe; 19 Aug 2024; [HN 41291251](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41291251), [HN 41294105](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41294105); named-handle individual who says "I work on this at Stripe" (bhuga); V-RAW. Quotes: "the laptops skew across literally years of versions of dev tools"; "the less that's on the laptop, the more control we have". Caveat: one voice; not a company post.
10. Discord: a `brew upgrade` could stop a developer's work, and the backend stack had trouble on Apple Silicon, which brought the move to remote Linux forward. Discord; 22 Feb 2024; [Discord](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-moved-engineering-to-cloud-development-environments); official, named author (Denbeigh Stevens); V-RAW.

### S1.2 Why attackers target the developer's machine: incidents

 1. Binance, 29 Oct 2026: about USD 6.34 billion in digital assets was affected; about USD 2.85 billion was frozen or intercepted as of the report. Binance; report shows actions "As of Nov 3, 2026"; [Binance, "Initial Findings and Response Update"](https://www.binance.com/en/blog/community/3229153443759566763); official; V-RAW (reader), amount range cross-checked in [The Register, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/03/north-korean-fake-job-interview-led-to-63b-binance-heist/5307398) (V-RAW). Caveat: an initial report; Binance says findings are subject to independent review and some technical details are withheld. Other figures in circulation: USD 6.3 billion (The Register), "approximately $6.4 billion" (Security Alliance).
 2. Binance, June 2026: "Engineer A" of Wallet DevOps downloaded a tampered Homebrew installation while trying to install a popular AI coding tool found through a search engine. Security detected it, replaced the device and rotated credentials, but the attacker had learned who worked on wallet infrastructure. Same report; official; V-RAW (reader), confirmed in The Register (V-RAW).
 3. Binance, September 2026: "Software Engineer B" was approached on a legitimate job-hunting platform; after a first video interview and a second interview by chat, he was told to download an assignment onto his work computer, and the repository ran malicious code during setup. Same report; official; V-RAW (reader). Quote: "Engineer B was instructed to download an “assignment” containing a malicious code repository onto his work computer." Caveat: Binance says "work computer" and "device", never "laptop"; the file paths it prints are under `/opt/homebrew`, which implies a Mac, but that is an inference.
 4. Binance: the malware replaced a legitimate development tool (the report's listing shows `pyenv` under Homebrew) with a malicious version and was not detected by endpoint protection. Same report; official; V-RAW (reader).
 5. Binance, 20 Oct 2026: from Engineer B's computer the attacker used an internal AI tool to gain control over Engineer A and a Wallet Software Engineer C, and found a container deployment permission token on Engineer A's computer. Same report; official; V-RAW (reader). Quote: "used an internal AI tool to gain control over the Wallet DevOps Engineer A and another Wallet Software Engineer C". Caveat: the report does not name the tool or say how it gave control.
 6. Binance, 29 Oct 2026: the attacker logged onto Engineer C's computer, used credentials stolen from Engineer A, deployed a container and triggered a script that reached the wallet signing service. Same report; official; V-RAW (reader). Quote (also in The Register): "It was not a single isolated breach or a simple phishing incident."
 7. Binance's stated response: rebuilt the wallet system and the internal AI tool, replaced affected devices, decommissioned the engineers' credentials and tokens, scanned all employee devices, and "implemented tighter device controls"; investment is promised in "developer-environment security". Same report; official; V-RAW (reader). Caveat: no statement about where development will happen.
 8. Security Alliance on Binance: the suspected root cause is a developer device compromised by malware delivered through a job-interview lure; the attack is attributed to TraderTraitor (also Jade Sleet, UNC4899), which the analysis treats as distinct from the Contagious Interview subgroup; dwell time about 8 weeks. Security Alliance (SEAL Intel); 5 Nov 2026; [SEAL Radar](https://radar.securityalliance.org/tradertraitor-vs-contagious-interview-a-look-at-binances-initial-incident-report/); non-profit security group, outside analysis; V-RAW. Quote: "companies should assume engineers will be compromised and prepare accordingly". Caveat: SEAL notes "Binance has not yet provided any meaningful technical indicators".
 9. Security Alliance's advice bearing on the essay: run any external code in a throwaway VM without corporate credentials, SSH keys or cloud sessions; remove standing local administrator rights from employee laptops; treat a developer's session as a privileged credential; and note that an attacker on the endpoint can keep stealing short-lived sessions as they refresh. Same link; V-RAW. Quote: "should run in a throwaway VM with no access to corporate credentials, SSH keys or cloud sessions".
10. Bybit and Safe, Feb 2025: Safe's statement says the attack on Bybit's Safe was achieved through a compromised Safe{Wallet} developer machine that led to a disguised malicious transaction being proposed. Safe; 26 Feb 2025; [Safe on X](https://x.com/safe/status/1894768522720350673) (an X article; title and preview seen through X's syndication endpoint, V-RAW), body quoted in [BleepingComputer, 26 Feb 2025](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/lazarus-hacked-bybit-via-breached-safe-wallet-developer-machine/); official; V-RAW (quoted in). Caveat: the full Safe text was not opened. Loss is given as USD 1.5 billion by BleepingComputer and The Hacker News and USD 1.4 billion by SecurityWeek.
11. Safe with Mandiant, 6 Mar 2025: a Safe developer's laptop ("Developer1") was compromised and AWS session tokens were hijacked to bypass multi-factor authentication; the developer had elevated access; the macOS machine was compromised on 4 Feb 2025 through a Docker project (`MC-Based-Stock-Invest-Simulator-main`); the attacker removed the malware and cleared shell history. Safe; 6 Mar 2025; [Safe on X, "Investigation Updates and Community Call to Action"](https://x.com/safe/status/1897663514975649938) (preview seen, V-RAW), body quoted in [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/03/safewallet-confirms-north-korean.html) and [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/how-social-engineering-sparked-a-billion-dollar-supply-chain-cryptocurrency-heist/); official with Mandiant; V-RAW (quoted in). Caveat: no separate Mandiant publication was found; the findings exist as Safe's statement. Session tokens, not files, were what the attacker used.
12. Safe's remediation included rotating all credentials and "provisioning new developer machines". Safe via [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/how-social-engineering-sparked-a-billion-dollar-supply-chain-cryptocurrency-heist/); press paraphrase of the official statement; V-RAW (quoted in). Caveat: paraphrase, not Safe's words.
13. GitHub, May 2026: one employee device was compromised through a poisoned VS Code extension published by a third party, and the attacker's claim of about 3,800 internal repositories was "directionally consistent" with GitHub's investigation. GitHub; 20 May 2026, updated 26 May; [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/security/investigating-unauthorized-access-to-githubs-internal-repositories/); official, CISO Alexis Wales; V-RAW. Quotes: "On Monday May 18, we detected and contained a compromise of an employee device involving a poisoned VS Code extension published by a third party."; "The attacker’s current claims of ~3,800 repositories are directionally consistent with our investigation so far."
14. GitHub's response as published: removed the extension version, isolated the endpoint, rotated critical secrets, and later rotated keys including the GitHub Enterprise Server signing key. Same link; official; V-RAW. Caveat: the post does not say whether the repositories were on the device or fetched with credentials from it, and announces no change in where development happens.
15. The extension was a malicious version of Nx Console, linked by GitHub to the TanStack npm supply-chain attack. [BleepingComputer, 20 May 2026, updated 21 May](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/github-confirms-breach-of-3-800-repos-via-malicious-vscode-extension/); press; V-RAW. Caveat: press report of a GitHub statement not seen directly.
16. Microsoft, fake interviews: the campaign has run since at least December 2022 and targets software developers; attackers take API tokens, cloud credentials, signing keys, wallets and password-manager data, and the backdoor collects keys, environment files and source code. Microsoft Threat Intelligence; 11 Mar 2026; [Microsoft Security Blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/03/11/contagious-interview-malware-delivered-through-fake-developer-job-interviews/); official (also a security vendor); V-RAW. Quote: "Threat actors harvest API tokens, cloud credentials, signing keys, cryptocurrency wallets, and password manager artifacts."
17. Microsoft's mitigation: do coding tests in an isolated, disposable environment and never on the primary work machine. Same link; V-RAW. Quote: "Use a dedicated, isolated environment for coding tests and take-home assignments (for example, a non-persistent virtual machine). Do not use a primary corporate workstation that has access to production credentials, internal repositories, or privileged cloud sessions."

### S1.3 Guidance and numbers

 1. US guidance (NSA, CISA, ODNI): "Compromised remote development systems" is a numbered threat scenario, meaning off-site developers on organization-supplied machines over VPN. Sept 2022; [ESF, Recommended Practices for Developers (PDF)](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/ESF_SECURING_THE_SOFTWARE_SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEVELOPERS.PDF); official; V-RAW (PDF). Caveat: "remote" here is the home laptop, the opposite sense from the essay's remote server.
 2. Same guidance recommends a jump host between the less secure remote machine and the protected development environment, development systems used for development only, and if possible no internet access from them. V-RAW (PDF). Quotes: "a system which acts as a portal between a less secured remote host and the protected development environment"; "No other activity such as email should be conducted for business nor personal use."
 3. Same guidance lists engineering workstations among build-pipeline systems to secure and says to segregate the engineering network from the corporate network. V-RAW (PDF). Caveat: the document's worry is tampering with code more than theft, and it never says "keep source off laptops".
 4. Verizon DBIR 2024, lost and stolen assets: 199 incidents, 181 with confirmed data disclosure, in a report covering 30,458 incidents and 10,626 breaches. [DBIR 2024 (PDF mirror)](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2024-dbir-data-breach-investigations-report.pdf); official report; V-RAW (PDF). Quote: "Devices are still much more likely to be lost than stolen. Laptops continue to be a risk for loss in particular." Caveat: 181 of 10,626 is about 1.7%, a division done for the notes and not a Verizon figure.
 5. Same pattern: data compromised is "Personal (97%)"; the controls Verizon lists are device encryption, lockout and remote wipe. V-RAW (PDF). Caveat: nothing in the pattern concerns source code or credentials.

### S1.4 Honest qualifications

 1. Many source-code thefts did not come from a laptop's disk: Slack (Dec 2022, employee tokens stolen and used on GitHub; a third-party vendor was compromised), Dropbox (2022, phishing; 130 repositories copied; remedy WebAuthn), Cloudflare (Nov 2023, credentials from the Okta breach; 76 repositories; over 5,000 credentials rotated), Microsoft (Mar 2022, one compromised account). [Slack](https://slack.com/blog/news/slack-security-update), [Dropbox](https://dropbox.tech/security/a-recent-phishing-campaign-targeting-dropbox), [Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/thanksgiving-2023-security-incident), [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2022/03/22/dev-0537-criminal-actor-targeting-organizations-for-data-exfiltration-and-destruction/); official; all four V-RAW. Quote (Microsoft): "Microsoft does not rely on the secrecy of code as a security measure".
 2. None of the breached companies read (GitHub, Binance, Safe, Slack, Dropbox, Cloudflare, Microsoft) names moving development off the endpoint as its remedy; they rotated secrets, replaced devices and tightened authentication. Derived from entries 17, 22, 24, 33; V-RAW for each underlying text.
 3. A remote server moves the files and not the session: an attacker on the workstation can wait and hijack the connection. 2 commenters; 2017 and 2019; [HN 14492884](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=14492884), [HN 21702446](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21702446); V-RAW. Quote: "If the workstation is compromised, it's over." Supported by entry 19 (sessions re-stolen as they refresh).
 4. Companies that moved to remote machines gave speed and consistency as the reason, not security: Slack 2022, Uber 2022, LinkedIn 2021, GitHub 2021, Discord 2024 (entries 1 to 5, 10). V-RAW. Caveat: Uber lists security scanning of images as one benefit among several.
 5. Whether the laptop is a top risk is disputed: one security practitioner called developer-laptop compromise "the least likely to happen" compared with web application flaws. 1 commenter (jvehent); 8 Feb 2016; [HN 11056163](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11056163); V-RAW. Weak single voice, kept as the only dissent found.

### S1 Not established

- That a lost or stolen laptop has ever led to a published source-code breach. None was found.
- That any security leader of an operating company ranks developer laptops as a top risk in their own words. Vendor statements only.
- That the Binance machines were laptops, that the attack was "Contagious Interview", which AI tool was abused, or how. Binance's report says "work computer", SEAL attributes it to TraderTraitor, and the tool is unnamed.
- "Withdrawals halted for nearly two days", "ten chains", and the fake-ads detail from the note's addendum: not in Binance's report as read. The FBI alert returned 403 and was not read; Binance's report links to it and says it attributes the incident to North Korea.
- That any breached company moved development to servers in response.
- That a remote server would have stopped any of these incidents. No source says so; the Binance path ran from one engineer's computer to others and then to a deployment token.
- Unit 42, Atlassian and Elastic write-ups of the interview campaign, the UNC4899 AirDrop case of March 2026, and DBIR 2025 infostealer percentages remain SNIPPET; leave out or re-open.

## S2. History

### S2.1 Google

 1. Google has used a distributed build with remote caching and remote execution since 2008 (ObjFS, Forge). Google; 2020; [Software Engineering at Google, ch. 18](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch18.html); official book; V-RAW.
 2. Most Google developers reach the monorepo through CitC, a cloud storage backend with a Linux-only FUSE filesystem; a workspace stores only modified files, on average fewer than 10. Google; CACM July 2016; [quoted at mjtsai.com](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2016/06/30/why-google-stores-billions-of-lines-of-code-in-a-single-repository/), original [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/why-google-stores-billions-of-lines-of-code-in-a-single-repository/) (403); official paper; V-RAW (quoted in). Quote: "Most developers access Piper through a system called Clients in the Cloud, or CitC, which consists of a cloud-based storage backend and a Linux-only FUSE file system." Caveat: "over 80% of Piper users today use CitC" was not seen (UNVERIFIED, ACM page blocked).
 3. Cider, a web editor, began around 2013 (the author marks the year uncertain); Cider V on the VS Code frontend was decided in 2020; the 2021 open beta had 5,000 engineers; by 2023, 80% of development in the main codebase happened in Cider V. Google, by a former tech lead; 9 May 2026; [Laurent Le Brun](https://laurent.le-brun.eu/blog/a-history-of-ides-at-google); named individual (at Google 2011 to 2024); V-RAW. Quote: "by 2023, 80% of the development in the main Google codebase happened in Cider V". Caveat: share of development, not of engineers.
 4. Google's virtual desktops ran on Ganeti on the corporate network; migration to Google Cloud began in early 2016 and moved approximately 20,000 users; over one-fourth of Googlers used virtual desktops; pager load fell 95%. Google; ACM Queue, 2018; [From Corp to Cloud (PDF)](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/4484.pdf); official paper; V-RAW (PDF). Caveat: the paper's wording is "datacenter-hosted virtual desktops".
 5. Only about half of users cared enough about their virtual desktop's disk to wait 24 hours for it to be moved. Same paper; V-RAW (PDF). Quote: "only about 50 percent of users cared enough about preserving their disks to wait 24 hours for the move to complete".
 6. Cloudtop was first made available to employees in 2017 and is not sold to customers. [CNBC, 12 Apr 2021](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/04/12/google-cloudtop-virtual-desktop-tool-for-employees-only.html); press; V-RAW.
 7. By Dec 2022 most Google employees relied on virtual desktops, reached by SSH or Chrome Remote Desktop. Google; 8 Dec 2022; [Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/googles-virtual-desktop-future); official, named author (Nick Yeager); V-RAW. Quote: "some employees access their virtual desktop instances via Secure Shell (SSH), while others use Chrome Remote Desktop". Caveat: "employees", not engineers; the post is on the Google Cloud marketing blog.
 8. April 2023: Cloudtop to be "the default desktop"; refreshes of laptops, desktops and monitors paused. [CNBC, 3 Apr 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/03/google-to-cut-down-on-employee-laptops-services-and-staplers-to-save.html); press, from internal documents; V-RAW. Caveat: a cost memo.

### S2.2 Meta

 1. Nov 2019: Facebook made VS Code its default editor; most development was done on individually reserved servers in its data centers, with VS Code installed on laptops. Facebook; 19 Nov 2019; [Facebook developer blog](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2019/11/19/facebook-microsoft-partnering-remote-development); official; V-RAW (reader). Quote: "most development is done directly on individually-reserved development servers that live within our data centers".
 2. Nov 2022: many developers use an on-demand devserver, taken and released each day, made workable by a persistent home drive and source control that restores state; Meta says it is faster than a dedicated machine because tools and caches are prepared. Meta; 15 Nov 2022; [Meta developer blog](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2022/11/15/meta-developers-workflow-exploring-tools-used-to-code/); official, named author (Neil Mitchell); V-RAW (reader). Quote: "Many developers at Meta have a MacBook, but actually do their coding on a Linux server."
 3. By 2023 the majority of Meta developers used OnDemand containers, which live about a day and a half. Meta; QCon New York 2023, published 19 Dec 2023; [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/meta-production-environment/); official talk (Henrique Andrade); V-RAW. Quotes: "the majority of our developers tend to use these development containerized environments"; "They live for a day and a half and are then disposed of."
 4. OnDemand existed years before Meta wrote about it: commenters describe it as in transition around 2021 and "seconds" to obtain. 3 commenters; 2021 to 2023; [HN 26243011](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26243011), [HN 36510954](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36510954), [HN 34048215](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34048215); V-RAW. Caveat: no launch year from any source.

### S2.4 The laptop-sync era and its end

 1. Stripe to about 2019: code ran on a per-developer cloud devbox while checkout and editor stayed on the laptop, joined by a file watcher and rsync; devboxes were disposable with warm spares. Stripe; written 21 May 2024 about 2012 to 2019; [Nelson Elhage](https://blog.nelhage.com/post/stripe-dev-environment/); named former employee; V-RAW. Caveat: the author disclaims describing Stripe today.
 2. Stripe, 2022: the devbox still held a copy "rsynced from the laptop"; switching branches triggered the worst case of sync; remote development was at "the early stages of a beta". Stripe; published on InfoQ 30 Sep 2022; [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/stripe-dev-env-infrastructure/); official talk (Soam Vasani); V-RAW.
 3. Stripe, 2024: code no longer kept on developer machines; the sync had been "a fairly constant source of headaches". 2 commenters with stated Stripe ties (nkohari, bhuga); 19 Aug 2024; [HN 41291111](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41291111), [HN 41291828](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41291828); V-RAW. Quote (bhuga): "Code is off of laptops and lives entirely on the dev server in many (but not all) cases."
 4. Stripe, 2026: most human-written code is produced in an IDE connected to a devbox over SSH. Stripe; 19 Feb 2026; [Stripe Dev Blog](https://stripe.dev/blog/minions-stripes-one-shot-end-to-end-coding-agents-part-2); official, named author (Alistair Gray); V-RAW.
 5. Slack, 2020: code was edited on the laptop and pushed to an EC2 dev environment by `slack sync-dev`; 550 dev environments by the end of 2019. Slack; 28 Apr 2020; [Slack Engineering](https://slack.engineering/development-environments-at-slack/); official; V-RAW.
 6. Slack, 2021 to 2022: remote development reached general availability in October 2021; 30% of webapp engineers had switched during beta and over 90% by the end of January 2022. Slack; 28 Jun 2022; [Slack Engineering](https://slack.engineering/remote-development-at-slack/); official; V-RAW. Quote: "by the end of January 2022 over 90% of our engineers had completely switched to the remote development workflow".
 7. Amazon also had a sync period: the creator of Cloud Desktop said in 2017 that much development happened on the laptop and was synchronized; a 2022 commenter describes an rsync-over-ssh tool. 2 commenters; [HN 15391051](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=15391051), [HN 32091232](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32091232); V-RAW. Caveat: no Amazon publication.

### S2.5 GitHub, LinkedIn, Uber, Discord, Shopify

 1. GitHub, Aug 2021: Codespaces became the default environment for github.com, with roughly one codespace per branch from a prebuilt pool. [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/infrastructure/githubs-engineering-team-moved-codespaces/); official; V-RAW. Quote: "roughly a 1:1 mapping between branches and codespaces".
 2. LinkedIn, Nov 2021: RDev instances in LinkedIn's private cloud, reached from IDEs over SSH, with the product checked out in a home volume. [LinkedIn Engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/cloud-computing/building-in-the-cloud-with-remote-development); official; V-RAW.
 3. Uber, Dec 2022: Devpod is a persistent container on Kubernetes; over 60% of Uber software engineers had adopted it by November 2022. [Uber Engineering](https://www.uber.com/en-US/blog/devpod-improving-developer-productivity-at-uber/); official; V-RAW (reader). Quote: "over 60% of Uber software engineers have adopted Devpods".
 4. Uber, the other side: engineers said most developers still built locally because of input lag, and the largest services were not developed in Devpods. 3 voices; 2022; [HN 34046771](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34046771), [HN 34048691](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34048691), [uber/h3-java#116](https://github.com/uber/h3-java/issues/116) (third not re-opened); V-RAW for the two HN comments.
 5. Discord, Feb 2024: all backend and infrastructure development moved to Linux cloud environments (bought from Coder), macOS backend development was deprecated, frontend work stayed on laptops, and Kubernetes containers were replaced by VMs. [Discord](https://discord.com/blog/how-discord-moved-engineering-to-cloud-development-environments); official; V-RAW.
 6. ### S3.5 Disk

  1. Persistent network volume under disposable compute, Stripe: stateful parts of the devbox were moved to a persistent EBS volume that is reattached to the user's new instance, so devboxes can be switched off and a developer can have several. Stripe; published 30 Sep 2022; [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/presentations/stripe-dev-env-infrastructure/); official talk; V-RAW. Quote: "The state of source code remains on the volume, which means it's now cost acceptable to give users lots of devboxes."

Same pattern elsewhere: Uber deletes an idle environment's container and converts its disk to low-cost storage until restart (entry 83); LinkedIn keeps the checkout in a home volume (entry 59); Discord preserves `/home` (entry 84); Amazon dev desktops in two measured cases have an EBS root volume sized independently of the instance (entry 95); the public PyTorch GPU platform gives a 100 GB EBS home with snapshots taken when the reservation ends ([gpu-dev CLI README](https://github.com/pytorch/ci-infra/blob/main/gpu-dev/cli-tools/gpu-dev-cli/README.md), public documentation, V-RAW).
  3. Checkout size: at Cruise two clones of the repository took more than a terabyte, and engineers kept asking for more disk. Cruise; 9 Dec 2022; [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/inside-tools-keep-engine-humming-cruise); sponsored article quoting Sireesha Sura, Software Engineering Manager; V-RAW. Quote: "If you have two clones of our repository, that’s more than a terabyte of disk space." Caveat: the speaker is Sura, confirmed on the page.
  4. Virtual checkout as the remedy: the successor team at General Motors built CASFS, a FUSE filesystem that mounts a terabyte-scale tree at once and fetches content as needed. General Motors; 18 Aug 2026; [EngFlow meetup write-up](https://blog.engflow.com/2026/08/18/four-war-stories-and-a-demo-seattle-build-meetup-2026--engflow/); vendor write-up of a talk by Seth Bunce; V-RAW. Quote: "CASFS mounts a source tree immediately, then retrieves and warms content to local NVMe as it is needed."
  5. Google's working tree takes no local disk because it is a FUSE mount (CitC), and build outputs live in a remote cache (entries 38, 39); a Googler confirms "doesn't take any disk space (except caching)". 1 commenter; 8 Aug 2023; [HN 37043602](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37043602); V-RAW. Git-hosted work at Google does fill disks: a Bazel cache filled a half-terabyte drive about weekly, and a Flutter checkout left 150 GB of debris on a Cloudtop. 2 public artefacts; 2021, 2022; [bazel#12630](https://github.com/bazelbuild/bazel/issues/12630), [flutter/devtools#4111](https://github.com/flutter/devtools/issues/4111); V-RAW.

Rebuild as first-line repair: Stripe's "pull master and re-create your devbox" (Elhage, entry 51, V-RAW); Instacart's "Throw it away and get a new machine" (entry 86, V-RAW); Google Cloudtop rebuilt twice by one engineer after bad updates ([HN 50397671](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50397671), V-RAW).
  8. Root on the server, none on the laptop: Security Alliance's post-Binance advice is to remove standing local administrator rights from employee laptops (entry 19, V-RAW). This is advice, not a described practice.
